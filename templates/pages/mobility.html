{{ tHTML "page.mobility.fourLINDASGraphsDescribeHow" "Four LINDAS graphs describe how people move around Switzerland. They are not four independent datasets — they stack. The service point register (DiDok) holds the places: every stop, station, quay and cable-car terminal in the country. NOVA holds the prices between those places. The Open Transport Data schema is the dictionary that defines the terms both of them use. Alongside them, the Federal Office of Civil Aviation publishes aviation statistics, which stand on their own." }}

{{ tHTML "page.mobility.novaDescribesSwissPublicTransport" "NOVA describes Swiss public-transport pricing, and it is by far the largest graph in this theme. It does not contain timetables or ticket prices you can look up directly; it contains the machinery that produces a price. That machinery comes in two flavours, which is the key to reading the class list below." }}

Zone-based pricing

{{ tHTML "page.mobility.usedByTheRegionalFare" "Used by the regional fare networks. Stops are grouped into a Zone, zones are collected into a ZoningPlan (a region may run several — one for season tickets, another for single journeys), and the price follows from how many zones you cross." }}

Direct-traffic pricing

{{ tHTML "page.mobility.usedForJourneysAcrossThe" "Used for journeys across the national network. A Relation is a priced connection between two stops, and a Preistabelle (price table) assigns the fare. With over half a million relations, this is where the bulk of the graph lives." }}
