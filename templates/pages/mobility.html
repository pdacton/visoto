About this theme

Four LINDAS graphs describe how people move around Switzerland. They are not four independent datasets — they stack. The service point register (DiDok) holds the places: every stop, station, quay and cable-car terminal in the country. NOVA holds the prices between those places. The Open Transport Data schema is the dictionary that defines the terms both of them use. Alongside them, the Federal Office of Civil Aviation publishes aviation statistics, which stand on their own.

The network: where you can go

Every stop in the register carries a means of transport, and the spread of those values tells the Swiss story better than any summary could. Buses dominate by an order of magnitude, as they do anywhere. What is distinctive is the third group below: cable cars, chairlifts, funiculars and rack railways are not tourist attractions filed away in a separate registry — they sit in the same national register as the trams of Zurich, with the same kind of identifier, because in Switzerland a cable car up a mountain is simply public transport.

What it costs: the NOVA fare model

NOVA describes Swiss public-transport pricing, and it is by far the largest graph in this theme. It does not contain timetables or ticket prices you can look up directly; it contains the machinery that produces a price. That machinery comes in two flavours, which is the key to reading the class list below.

Zone-based pricing

Used by the regional fare networks. Stops are grouped into a Zone, zones are collected into a ZoningPlan (a region may run several — one for season tickets, another for single journeys), and the price follows from how many zones you cross.

Direct-traffic pricing

Used for journeys across the national network. A Relation is a priced connection between two stops, and a Preistabelle (price table) assigns the fare. With over half a million relations, this is where the bulk of the graph lives.

Fare networks

An Alliance (Verbund) is a regional fare union — the body whose zone plan applies where you are travelling. Most people in Switzerland hold a season ticket issued by one of these.

SELECT ?alliance ?label WHERE { GRAPH <https://lindas.admin.ch/sbb/nova> { ?alliance a <https://lod.opentransportdata.swiss/vocab/Alliance> . OPTIONAL { ?alliance rdfs:label ?label } } } ORDER BY ?label

By air: civil aviation

The Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA / BAZL) publishes its statistics as data cubes. The graph holds eighteen of them, but that number overstates the variety: they are re-published versions of just three subjects. The cards below point at the current version of each.

Air traffic movements

Annual movements per aerodrome, by category. One movement is a single take-off or landing — a return flight counts as two.

Passengers

Passengers at aerodromes and mountain landing sites — the latter a category of airfield few countries would need.

Passenger rights

Reports filed by air passengers each year. FOCA pursues those within its jurisdiction; unsigned or incomplete reports close automatically after six months.

Earlier versions of each cube remain published and can be reached from the cube list; an older Air traffic statistics movements series is also present.

The vocabulary

The Open Transport Data schema is tiny — a few hundred triples — but it defines the terms that the seven million triples of NOVA are written in. It is the Rosetta Stone for everything above.

SELECT ?class ?label ?comment WHERE { GRAPH <https://lindas.admin.ch/sbb/otd-schema> { ?class a rdfs:Class . OPTIONAL { ?class rdfs:label ?label } OPTIONAL { ?class rdfs:comment ?comment . FILTER (STR(?comment) != "") } } } ORDER BY ?class
