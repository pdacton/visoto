{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.intro" "Three federal offices publish here, and between them they follow food along its whole journey. The Federal Office for Agriculture tracks the market — what a product fetches at each stage from the farm gate to the till. The Food Safety and Veterinary Office covers the two things that can go wrong: pests in the field and disease in the barn. Agroscope, the federal agricultural research institute, contributes a small research series." }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.valueChainIdea" "The organising idea across the market data is the value chain. The same product is priced separately at production, at wholesale and processing, at catering wholesale, and at consumption — plus import and export. That is what makes this theme unusual: for milk, eggs or a kilo of carrots you can follow the same good through each hand it passes and see where the margin sits, rather than comparing a farm price with a shop price from two unrelated sources." }}

{{ t "page.agriculture.milk" "The milk price, at home and abroad" }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.milkIntro" "Milk is the best-instrumented product in the theme, and its monthly producer-price cube carries something unusual: alongside the Swiss figures it holds the same month's price in Germany, France, Austria, Italy, the EU as a whole, the United States and New Zealand. The international series are ordinary products in the same cube, so a domestic price and its foreign benchmark can be compared without leaving the dataset." }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.milkSegments" "The Swiss rows are split by segment — A for milk sold into protected domestic markets, B for exposed export products, C for the residual sold at world prices. The three trade at genuinely different levels, and the gap between them is the point of the segmentation." }}

{{ t "page.agriculture.eggs" "Eggs, and what husbandry costs" }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.eggsIntro" "Eggs make the clearest case for the production system dimension. The same size of egg is priced separately for organic, free-range and barn husbandry, and the spread between them is large and stable — roughly double from the cheapest system to organic. {{ t "page.agriculture.plantProtection" "What is licensed to be sprayed" }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.plantProtectionIntro" "The plant protection register is the complete list of pesticides a Swiss farmer may legally apply, and it is a register rather than a statistic — every authorised product, its federal admission number, the company holding the permission, its active substances and the exact crop-and-pest combinations it is cleared for. It is the one dataset in this theme with real regulatory force: if a product is not here, using it is illegal." }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.plantProtectionStructure" "The structure repays a moment's attention. A product carries indications — thousands of them — and each indication is a specific triple of crop, pest and dose, which is why the register holds several times more indications than products. Withdrawn products are not deleted but given a soldOutDeadline and an exhaustionDeadline: the dates by which stock must leave the shelves and by which remaining stock must be used up. That makes the register a record of the phase-out as well as the permission." }}

# ?type stays an IRI so the backend resolves and links each product category. # ?companies counts the distinct permission holders per category — a rough measure # of how competitive that corner of the market is. {{ t "page.agriculture.animalHealth" "Disease in the barn" }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.animalDiseaseIntro" "The animal disease dataset is the most immediate thing in this theme: a case register in which every row is one reported outbreak on one holding, with the diagnosis date, the animal species, the canton and municipality, and counts of how many animals in the stock were sick, infected, put down or died. It is updated continuously — the most recent diagnoses are usually only days old." }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.bluetongueStory" "Query it by disease and the recent history of Swiss livestock falls out of the data. The bluetongue epidemic that began in August 2024 dominates everything else by an order of magnitude and reached essentially every canton; foot rot follows, reflecting the national eradication programme that obliges holdings to report it. Further down sit the endemic background diseases that tick over at a few dozen cases a year." }}

# ?epidemic stays an IRI so the backend resolves the disease name and links it. # # diagnosis-date is typed xsd:date, NOT a plain string — a bare "2024-01-01" in the # FILTER matches nothing at all and the table comes back silently empty. The typed # literal is required. FILTER (?d >= "2024-01-01"^^xsd:date) } } GROUP BY ?epidemic } } ORDER BY DESC(?cases)

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.cantonCountCaveat" "The canton column can read 27 rather than 26: the register uses a separate code for the Principality of Liechtenstein, which participates in the Swiss veterinary area. {{ t "page.agriculture.referenceData" "The vocabularies underneath" }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.cropsIntro" "Two graphs in this theme hold no measurements at all. Crops is a genuine OWL ontology of Swiss cultivated plants — several hundred cultivation types arranged by rdfs:subClassOf, with owl:disjointWith axioms asserting that a plot cannot be two things at once, and deprecated classes retained so that older records still resolve. Crucially it is faceted: the same plant appears as a plant-protection crop, a nutrient-balance crop and a direct-payment crop, because the three regulatory regimes cut the plant world along different lines. Inspections is the corresponding checklist — the individual points an inspector verifies on a farm visit, grouped into collections." }}

{{ tHTML "page.agriculture.cropsLabelCaveat" "Crop names carry no language tag: Zuckerrüben, Betteraves sucrières and Barbabietole da zucchero are three untagged literals on one resource, and there is no English at all. A crop may therefore surface under whichever spelling the resolver reaches first, and language filtering with lang() will discard every one of them. The same pattern appears in the plant-protection substance names." }}
